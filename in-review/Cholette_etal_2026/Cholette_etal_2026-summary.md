## Overview

The paper adds two prognostic aerosol number mixing ratios to P3, water-friendly (CCN-like, `Na,w`) and ice-friendly (INP-like, `Na,i`), following Thompson and Eidhammer (2014). This is P3 v6.0. The aerosols are activated, nucleated, scavenged and regenerated by evaporation and sublimation. The change is tested in a single 24-h 1-km GEM simulation of a layered Arctic mixed-phase cloud near Inuvik on 19–20 January 2026 (PONEX campaign). Simulations are evaluated against Inuvik surface data, soundings and MRR-PRO radar, GOES-18, and an EarthCARE overpass at 00 UTC 20 January. After revision the main finding is that the differences between the aerosol and baseline runs come mostly from the change of ice-nucleation scheme (Cooper 1986 → aerosol-dependent DeMott) that comes with the aerosol option, not from the aerosols being prognostic.

## Context and Motivation

- Baseline P3 uses a fixed CCN concentration (300 × 10⁶ kg⁻¹ in Khvorostyanov–Curry activation) and no INP dependence at all: ice nucleation is Cooper (1986), a function of temperature only.
- Arctic mixed-phase clouds are used as the test case because their phase partitioning and persistence are controlled by INP and CCN, and models tend to over-glaciate them.
- Operational NWP systems (IFS, GFS, UM-UKCA, RAQDPS) treat aerosols mainly through their direct radiative effect. Indirect effects in convection-permitting bulk schemes are partial: Thompson–Eidhammer in WRF/HRRR, CMA-MESO, WRF-Chem.
- P3 runs operationally in the HRDPS and CAPS, so an aerosol-aware P3 is a step toward aerosol–cloud coupling in ECCC's km-scale systems.

## Key Scientific Contributions

- **P3 v6.0 prognostic-aerosol option**, switched on and off at run time; with it off the code reverts to v5.5. It adds:
  - `Na,w`, `Na,i` initialised from the GOCART monthly climatology (Colarco et al. 2010; 2001–2007, 0.5° × 1.25°). Dust > 0.5 µm becomes `Na,i`; the other species except black carbon become `Na,w`.
  - A surface source: relaxation of the lowest level to the climatology with a 1.5-h time scale. The Thompson–Eidhammer emission made the lowest level grow too fast.
  - ARG (Abdul-Razzak and Ghan 2000) droplet activation from w + (2/3 TKE)^½ with prognostic `Na,w` (KC with prognostic `Na,w` is also available).
  - Aerosol-dependent ice nucleation (DeMott; Tobo et al. 2013 also implemented), still applied only at T < −15 °C and ice supersaturation > 5 %.
  - A **new cloud-droplet number sink from evaporation**, `Nc,evp = qc,evp·Nc/qc` (absent from baseline P3), which returns aerosol to `Na,w`.
  - Aerosol scavenging by rain and by ice (Slinn–Shen/Wang et al. 2010 efficiencies; the ice rates go in the lookup table, computed at −20 °C).
  - Homogeneous freezing of deliquesced aerosol (Koop et al. 2000).
  - Contact/immersion freezing of droplets and rain as `Na,i` sinks. This was added in the revision.
- **Appendix A** is the first published record of the changes from v5.3 to v5.5: Hallett–Mossop rime splintering for nCat = 1 (Fr ≥ 0.5, Fl ≤ 0.1, Dm > 0.25 mm, lowest-level T < 9 °C) and a lookup table for µ and ρ in the three-moment ice configuration.
- **Attribution result.** Swapping C86 back into the aerosol configuration (AERO-C86) reproduces BASE almost everywhere: liquid-layer coverage, OLR, CWP/IWP, precipitation and EarthCARE-track profiles. The only exception is droplet effective radius, which comes from `Nc,evp`. The prescribed INP number in DeMott (15 × 10⁴ → 15 × 10⁶ kg⁻¹) moves the result monotonically. CCN treatment (KC vs ARG, prognostic vs fixed `Na,w`) matters little in this weak-ascent (< 1 m s⁻¹) case.

## Methods Summary

- GEM 1 km (0.01°), 84 levels, Δt 30 s, 24 h from 03 UTC 19 Jan 2026, nested in CAPS (3 km, 62 levels, P3 v3.1). P3 v6.0 with one ice category, three-moment ice and predicted liquid fraction. Aerosols affect only microphysics (no aerosol radiative effect).
- BASE: KC with fixed CCN 300 × 10⁶ kg⁻¹, C86 ice nucleation. AERO: ARG with prognostic `Na,w`, DeMott with prognostic `Na,i`.
- Sensitivity runs (Table 2):
  - BASE-DM15_Nai1/2/3 and AERO-DM15_Nai1/2/3: DeMott with fixed INP 15 × 10⁴/10⁵/10⁶ kg⁻¹, used only inside the nucleation calculation.
  - AERO-C86, AERO-T13, AERO-KC, AERO-KC_NawFix.
  - In Appendix B: AERO-clean/-polluted (initial aerosol ×0.5/×2), AERO-no_Sfc_Emission, AERO-no_Nc,evp.
- Evaluation:
  - GOES-18 10.3 µm vs model OLR.
  - Six-hourly Inuvik soundings, WXT536 surface time series, MRR-PRO reflectivity (nearest grid point).
  - EarthCARE ACM-CAP (Baseline BC) reflectivity, IWC, CWC and effective radii along frame 09352C: two-dimensional sections plus a new track-mean profile figure (Fig. 14).
  - Liquid-layer coverage: the fraction of each layer (0–3, 3–4, 4–5, 5–6 km) with CWC > 0.

## Key Results

- Large-scale cloud placement is the same in both runs and matches GOES. Precipitation is small (< 5 mm) and barely changes.
- AERO has higher OLR (+10–45 W m⁻²) and cloud tops 2–6 °C warmer (BASE reaches −30 °C at 450–550 hPa southeast of Inuvik, AERO −27 °C at 550–650 hPa).
- Along the EarthCARE track, AERO has ice number about one order of magnitude lower, larger and more rimed ice, and reflectivity 6–20 dBZ higher. IWC is lower, which brings the track mean closer to the retrieval but still above it. AERO keeps more supercooled liquid above 3 km: some of the embedded layers in the post-frontal "type A" cloud (still misplaced) and the cloud-top liquid layer in the Arctic stratiform "type B" cloud with generating cells.
- Both runs overestimate ice effective radius, and AERO makes it worse above 6 km. Droplet effective radius is overestimated by ×1.9 (BASE) and ×2.8 (AERO). Mean r_eff,c is 9.4 (BASE), 9.9 (AERO without `Nc,evp`) and 14.2 µm (AERO), so `Nc,evp` causes most of the increase.
- The OLR increase is attributed to fewer, larger ice crystals (optically thinner ice cloud) under DeMott. AERO-C86 removes it.
- T13 behaves like DeMott but with a smaller ice-number reduction, and gives the most liquid at 3–4 km. Liquid-layer occurrence above 3 km rises as the fixed `Na,i` falls. BASE and AERO with the same fixed-INP DeMott give similar liquid layers and ice properties; their differences are in droplets (KC vs ARG, `Nc,evp`).
- Halving or doubling the initial aerosol, removing surface emission, aerosol scavenging and aerosol homogeneous freezing (T never < −35 °C) all have small effects in this case.
- The surface is about 5 °C too cold at Inuvik in all runs. RH_w is 2–10 % low and pressure about 5 hPa high.

## Limitations and Caveats

Acknowledged in the paper:

- One 24-h case with weak ascent. Preliminary multi-case results (1-km PONEX runs, 60 HRDPS 2.5-km winter-2022 cases) are "not shown". The authors' response says the HRDPS figure used an outdated configuration.
- The aerosol state is a 2001–2007 climatology with no event-specific constraint. The conclusions now say "prognostic aerosols" means prognostic *processing*, not a realistic aerosol prediction. The 1.5-h relaxation time scale is called "somewhat arbitrary".
- No aerosol radiative effect. Vertical resolution and WBF sensitivity are not explored.

Noted while reading revision 1 (for the co-author read; not raised by the reviewers unless stated):

1. **The DeMott citation appears inverted, and probably T13 as well.** The response to Reviewer 2 relabels DM10 → DM15 throughout. But Eq. (B12) has a = 5.94 × 10⁻⁵, b = 3.33, c = 0.0264, d = 0.0033, which are the DeMott et al. (2010) coefficients. P3 `dev-aerosols` (`918356d`) uses the same numbers with the comment "DeMott et al. (2010)". Eq. (B13), labelled Tobo et al. (2013), has the form and coefficients of DeMott et al. (2015): exponent 0.46(273.16 − T) − 11.6, power 1.25, with the calibration factor cf set to 1 instead of 3. The code's commented-out "Tobo" line has the same constants. As I recall the Tobo et al. (2013) fit, it has different coefficients (α ≈ −0.074, β ≈ 3.8, γ ≈ 0.414, δ ≈ −9.671). Check these coefficients against the three papers before the resubmission. The version of the code used for the revision may differ from `dev-aerosols`.
2. **Abstract and first conclusions paragraph still credit the prognostic aerosols.** They say "the inclusion of prognostic aerosols significantly affects cloud microphysical properties", while Sect. 5 shows that DeMott vs C86 accounts for these differences. This is Reviewer 2's main point, and the abstract text is largely unchanged.
3. Primary ice nucleation (all schemes) is only active at T < −15 °C. This matters for supercooled layers at −5 to −15 °C, and the paper does not discuss it. The code also caps C86 at 100 L⁻¹ (`min(dum,100.e3*i_rho*SCF)`), which Eq. (B11) does not show.
4. W in Appendix B2 is written `min(0.1, w + (2/3 TKE)^½)` but described as "a minimum set to 0.1 m s⁻¹"; it should be max.
5. Leftovers from the revision:
   - Sect. 1's outline still says Sect. 5 is the conclusions.
   - "two cloud layers … simulated in the 1-km runs … simulated in all runs" (Sect. 4.3) is left over from the removed CAPS comparison.
   - Sect. 5.2.2 says "Fixing `Na,i`" for AERO-KC_NawFix; it should be `Na,w`.
   - Sect. 2.2 repeats the AERO-C86/AERO-T13 sentence.
   - Fig. 4 cross-reference: IWC is "Fig. 4a–b" and should be 4d–e (Reviewer 1 flagged a neighbouring one).
6. Figure captions do not match the text:
   - Fig. 14: the caption lists nine panels including specific humidity and temperature; the text uses b = IWC … g = r_eff,c.
   - Fig. 20: the caption skips (c)–(d).
   - Fig. 7: (a), (b), (d).
   - The Fig. 14 track-mean reflectivity "−11.8, −12.6 and 15.8" is missing a sign.
7. Numbers that disagree within the paper:
   - The ice effective radius increase is 5–10 µm (Sect. 4.3 text) but 10–30 µm (its summary).
   - IWC "0.5 to 1 g m⁻³ lower" in thin ice cloud looks an order of magnitude too large.
   - The Hallett–Mossop splinter diameter is "10 mm" and should be 10 µm.
   - R = 8.3187 J mol⁻¹ kg⁻¹ should be 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.
   - The mean r_eff,c values are given to four decimals.

## Relation to Author's Research Program

- The paper documents the prognostic-aerosol option of P3 6.0 (`dev-aerosols`), which runs in EXP1KM (P3 6.0.0-rc6 since 2026-10-27). In EXP1KM the option was configured but inert until the climatology reached the model on 2026-10-09 (exp1km L-92, gem L-64). Which activation and nucleation options EXP1KM uses has not been checked here. The revision's "small bug found in the ARG parameterization", which made all AERO runs be redone, matches `dev-aerosols` commits `55f8dfe` / `79d3c5c` (rc3, "Hugh's ARG activation fix").
- It is the publication record for the P3 v5.3 → v5.5 changes (Appendix A) and the v6.0 aerosol option, extending [[Cholette_etal_2019-summary]], [[Cholette_etal_2023-summary]], [[Cholette_etal_2024-summary]] and Morrison et al. (2025).
- Its scientific result (INP number controls supercooled-liquid persistence and over-glaciation in P3) is the same question as the mixed-phase-clouds project, where P3's f∞ is far below Korolev's aircraft value, lowest when cold (Korolev and Milbrandt 2022). That project is a natural place to test whether DeMott nucleation raises f∞.
- Both co-providers of data (Qu, Dedekind) are the RTTOV/EarthCARE contacts of the rttov-p3 work.
