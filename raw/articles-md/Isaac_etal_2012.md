## 1. Introduction

The Vancouver 2010 Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games (12 February–21 March 2010) provided an opportunity for an international collaborative research and forecast demonstration project. The WWRP (World Weather Research Programme) endorsed the Science of Nowcasting Olympic Weather for Vancouver 2010 (SNOW-V10) as an official Research Development Project (RDP) — the first WWRP project focused on nowcasting winter weather in complex terrain.

Predecessor projects include the Sydney 2000 and Beijing 2008 summer Olympics, which were mainly Forecast Demonstration Projects (FDPs). SNOW-V10 was broader in scope, combining research to understand high impact winter weather processes in complex terrain with real-time nowcast demonstration.

---

## 2. Objectives of SNOW-V10

- Improve understanding and ability to nowcast low cloud and visibility
- Improve understanding and ability to nowcast precipitation amount and type
- Improve nowcasts of wind speed, gusts, and direction
- Develop improved nowcast production systems for winter applications
- Assess and evaluate value of nowcasts to end users
- Increase capacity of WMO member states to provide nowcasts (training)

Participants from Canada, USA, Australia, China, Austria, Finland, Germany, and Switzerland.

---

## 3. Instrumentation

Approximately 56 auto stations, 14 moveable tripod stations, and 22 web camera sites (>100 weather observation sites total). Key specialized instruments included:
- Whistler (VVO) scanning C-band radar
- NOAA/OU X-band dual-polarization radar (NOX)
- Vertically pointing X-band radars at TFL, RND, WWA
- 915 MHz boundary layer wind profiler at Squamish Airport
- Profiling microwave radiometer at Timing Flats
- Rawinsonde system at Nesters
- Ceilometers, recording visibility meters, micro rain radars (MRR)
- Parsivel disdrometers for precipitation type and intensity
- Continuous snow photography at RND

---

## 5. Real-Time Forecast Support

A Research Support Desk (RSD) staffed by 2–3 scientists in Vancouver provided real-time scientific advice to venue forecasters. WebEx technology connected the SNOW-V10 Science Team across Canada and other countries. Forecasters were co-located with venue managers for critical decision-making regarding postponement thresholds (e.g., wind, visibility, new snow depth).

---

## 6. Verification

Verification used multi-categorical Heidke Skill Score (HSS). Key findings:
- Canadian LAM 1 km model: HSS = 0.275 for relative humidity, 0.104 for visibility, 0.54 for precipitation type at Whistler Mountain Mid Station (VOL)
- Model tended to under-forecast relative humidity when observed RH > 94%
- Low visibilities (50–200 m) were poorly captured
- Model forecast more frozen precipitation than observed
- Human forecasters had better mean absolute error than NWP models for temperature and wind gust in the first few hours; NWP performance improved at longer lead times
- INTW nowcast system had lowest MAE in relative humidity, even beating persistence at most sites

---

## 7. Key Findings and Summary

- Several important physical phenomena were documented: diabatic effects of melting snow, wind flow around/over terrain, diurnal flow reversal in valleys, precipitation modulation by terrain
- High temporal resolution (1–15 min) measurements were essential given the rapid variability of mountain weather
- Co-location of research and operational teams proved successful
- Nowcasting techniques that blend observations and NWP (e.g., INTW, INCA) outperformed raw NWP for short lead times
- Persistence was difficult to beat for very short lead times (0–2 h)
- All models struggled with relative humidity and wind direction in complex terrain
- The unique observational dataset and lessons from SNOW-V10 are intended as a resource for WMO member states to improve winter weather forecasting
