# Dawson, D.T. II, M. Xue, A. Shapiro, J.A. Milbrandt, and A.D. Schenkman, 2016: Sensitivity of Real-Data Simulations of the 3 May 1999 Oklahoma City Tornadic Supercell and Associated Tornadoes to Multimoment Microphysics. Part II: Analysis of Buoyancy and Dynamic Pressure Forces in Simulated Tornado-Like Vortices. *J. Atmos. Sci.*, **73**, 1039–1065. DOI: 10.1175/JAS-D-15-0114.1

---

## Abstract

Two high-resolution (250-m grid spacing) real-data simulations of the 3 May 1999 Oklahoma City tornadic supercell, using single-moment (MY1A) and triple-moment (MY3) configurations of the Milbrandt–Yau (MY) multimoment bulk microphysics scheme, are analyzed. The TLV (tornado-like vortex) in MY3 is substantially more intense and longer-lived than in MY1A. Triple-moment microphysics produces less rain/hail mass in low levels and larger rain drop sizes, leading to less latent cooling and warmer outflow. Trajectory analysis shows that more MY3 TLV inflow parcels originate from dynamically induced descent, while buoyant descent dominates in MY1A. Pressure buoyancy (from the negative pressure perturbations within the TLV itself) is found to be of prime importance within the TLV, compensating for slight negative thermal buoyancy in the triple-moment case.

---

## 1. Introduction

Part II extends the analysis from Part I (Dawson et al. 2015), which established that higher-moment microphysics produces weaker cold pools and stronger, longer-lived simulated tornadoes. Part II focuses on the vertical momentum budget within the TLV: specifically, the relative contributions of thermal buoyancy, pressure buoyancy, and dynamic vertical pressure gradient acceleration (VPPGA) to TLV intensification and maintenance.

---

## 2. Methods

### Model

ARPS (Advanced Regional Prediction System; Xue et al. 2001, 2003). Triply nested grids: 3-km, 1-km, 250-m horizontal spacing. Radar and high-resolution observation data assimilation on 1-km grid (10-min intervals, 2100–2250 UTC). 250-m simulations initialized at 2305 UTC from interpolated 1-km forecasts.

### Microphysics Experiments

| Label | Scheme | Description |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| MY1A | Milbrandt–Yau single-moment | Default Marshall-Palmer rain intercept; high evaporation; strong cold pool |
| MY3 | Milbrandt–Yau triple-moment | Three moments predicted for rain/hail; low evaporation; weak cold pool |

### Analysis Methods

- Vertical momentum equation decomposed into: thermal buoyancy, pressure buoyancy (perturbation pressure contribution to buoyancy), dynamic VPPGA
- Backward and forward trajectory analyses to trace inflow air origins relative to TLV
- Hydrometeor loading and latent heat diagnostics along trajectories

---

## 3. Key Results

### Cold Pool Differences

- MY1A: small rain drops (O(1 mm)), large rain/hail amounts in low levels → strong evaporative cooling → strong cold pool → negatively buoyant TLV inflow
- MY3: large rain drops (O(3 mm)), less rain/hail in low levels → weak evaporative/melt cooling → weak cold pool → neutral or slightly negative thermal buoyancy in TLV inflow

### TLV Behavior

- MY3 TLV: substantially more intense, longer-lived — consistent with observed F5 tornado
- MY1A TLV: weak and short-lived; strong negative thermal buoyancy suppresses vortex stretching

### Trajectory Analysis

- MY3: majority of TLV inflow from largely unmodified environmental inflow; dynamically induced descent in RFD with minimal rain evaporation/hail melting
- MY1A: greater percentage of inflow descending through large depth; much more evaporative cooling along trajectories

### Buoyancy Budget

- In both experiments: upward dynamic VPPGA dominates in the lowest few hundred m AGL
- Pressure buoyancy (strong negative pressure perturbations in TLV core) provides substantial positive buoyancy — a key previously under-quantified term in tornado dynamics
- MY3: pressure buoyancy overcomes slight negative thermal buoyancy → TLV intensification
- MY1A: pressure buoyancy too weak to overcome strong negative thermal buoyancy → TLV weakens

---

## 5. Summary

Triple-moment microphysics produces larger rain drops, less evaporative/melt cooling, warmer outflow, and weaker cold pools → stronger, longer-lived simulated tornadoes. The pressure buoyancy term within the TLV itself plays a critical role in vertical acceleration and TLV maintenance, particularly in the triple-moment case. This study provides the first quantitative vertical momentum budget analysis for tornado-like vortices in real-data simulations.
