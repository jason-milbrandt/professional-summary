## 1. Introduction

The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) founded the World Weather Research Programme (WWRP) in 1998, with a Scientific Steering Committee, six working groups, and one expert team. In 2014, the Nowcasting and Mesoscale Research (NMR) Working Group merged from two existing groups. Nowcasting is defined as the detailed description of the current state of the atmosphere and prediction of changes on a timescale of a few hours (0–2 h) by any method. Very short-range forecasting (VSRF) extends this to less than 6 h.

The NMR objectives were to advance the science of nowcasting, promote nowcasting to WMO members, and build capacity. Severe weather warnings are critical: in Canada, tornadoes had a probability of detection of only 35%, with lead times of at least 10 min only 20% of the time. In the United States (2016–2020), average tornado warning lead time was 8–10 min, with little change over two decades. Economic losses from medium-severity natural disaster events have grown at 7.5% per year, reaching $64B in insured losses in 2023.

The paper summarizes insights from Forecast Demonstration Projects (FDPs), Research and Development Projects (RDPs), training workshops, and symposia from 2000 to 2024.

## 2. Systems and Information Flow

FDPs involve real-time aspects, statistical verification, and demonstration of societal impacts. RDPs address particular scientific challenges (e.g., fog, snow microphysics, high-resolution NWP initialization). In Olympic FDPs, special forecast systems are set up to serve venue managers, sport judges, and emergency managers, with venue forecasters (VFs) collocated with decision-makers and briefed as frequently as every 10–15 min in critical situations.

## 3. Project Summaries

### Olympic and Sporting Events

| Acronym | Project | Focus |
|---------|---------|-------|
| S2K | Sydney 2000 FDP | Summer severe weather demonstration |
| B08 | Beijing 2008 FDP/RDP | Summer convective weather, 3D radar mosaic |
| V2010 | Vancouver 2010 FDP/RDP | Winter complex-terrain nowcasting |
| FROST-2014 | Sochi 2014 FDP/RDP | High-resolution NWP in complex terrain; multimodel ensembles |
| ICE-POP | PyeongChang 2018 RDP | Winter microphysics; research aircraft and ship-borne observations |

**Vancouver 2010 (V2010):** A key challenge was nowcasting in complex mountain terrain for both alpine and coastal venues. ECCC contributed an experimental high-resolution forecast system (Mailhot et al. 2014), with hectometric NWP and a 1D micronowcasting system (Isaac et al. 2014b). Nowcasts were provided for specific venue grid points matched to slope direction and height rather than the nearest grid point.

**FROST-2014:** Explored the added value of high-resolution NWP in complex terrain using nested model configurations. Multimodel forecasts adjusted to the latest observations served as first-guess guidance to venue forecasters.

**ICE-POP:** First RDP focused on improving winter microphysics using research aircraft, ship-based observations, and advanced international observing systems.

### Urban and Interdisciplinary Projects

- **AvRDP (Aviation RDP):** Collaboration between WMO and ICAO to develop nowcasting for airport terminal operations (0–6 h); Phase 2 added probabilistic/AI methods.
- **INCA-CE:** Transnational regional FDP (Central Europe) promoting international collaboration and codesigned nowcasting services for public safety and civil protection.
- **TOMACS:** Tokyo urban convection study with rapid-scan multibeam radars, decametric modeling, and social experiment on public displays of nowcasts.
- **ECPASS:** Demonstrated the full Integrated Urban Services concept integrating weather, air quality, water, and health at hectometric scale.

### Capacity Building

Four training workshops, four Nowcasting Symposia, and a developer workshop (T-NOTE 2011) were conducted. The Beijing 2008 project included more than ten workshops (~200 trainees).

## 4. Insights

### a. End-User Decision-Making and Service Requirements
- Nowcasts were initially underestimated by decision-makers but became the first agenda item in critical planning activities.
- Decision-making is complex and may not align explicitly with stated meteorological requirements or objective meteorological conditions.
- Scope expanded from summer convective hazards to winter, aviation, urban, air quality, hydrology, and health.

### b. Nowcast Systems
- Progression from purely observation-based extrapolation systems to NWP–observation blended systems, interactive systems, and AI-based methods.
- Stochastic radar ensembles and probabilistic nowcasting showed added value for communicating uncertainty.
- Verification was essential: the B08 project developed a real-time verification system (Ebert and Brown 2007) that quantified nowcast skill objectively.

### c. Modeling and Data Assimilation
- High-resolution hectometric models exposed gaps in scientific knowledge of small-scale processes.
- The transition from observational nowcasting to NWP-blended VSRF required bridging a fundamental gap between observation-based systems (0–120 min extrapolation) and mesoscale NWP (48 h to <2 h).
- Complex terrain remains a major challenge for NWP-based VSRF.

### d. Verification
- Coarse-resolution precipitation nowcasts sometimes verified favorably against hourly surface observations, masking true spatial skill.
- Nowcast systems with high temporal/spatial resolution (5–10 min, few km) were at a disadvantage against coarser verification networks.
- Intercomparison of nowcast systems in common weather regimes was an efficient and fair approach that reduced duplication of effort.

### e. Science and Knowledge Gaps
- Convective initiation and storm evolution remain the most fundamental scientific challenges.
- The community is returning to experimental and theoretical studies of microphysics (Morrison et al. 2020).
- Urban boundary layer and land surface processes need improved treatment at decametric scales.

### f. Observations
- Alternative and third-party data sources (mobile phones, IoT, crowd-sourced) and satellite/lightning-based nowcasting are important particularly for data-sparse regions.
- Affordability of observational networks is a systemic operational challenge, especially for developing countries.

### g. Technology Transfer (Research to Operations)
- FDPs were critical to technology transfer by providing early adopters the opportunity to evaluate and learn from new technology from enthusiasts.
- The FDP model was highly effective for mentoring young scientists and operational staff and building a community of nowcasters.
- High-resolution research nowcast products were uniformly adopted after FDP demonstration.

---

## 5. Summary

Over 25 years, the NMR Working Group advanced nowcasting through FDPs, RDPs, training, and symposia. Key advances include:
- Progression from observation-based extrapolation to NWP-observation blended and AI-based systems
- Expanded scope from summer convection to winter weather, aviation, urban, and interdisciplinary domains
- Hectometric-scale NWP and stochastic ensemble nowcasting
- Verification frameworks that quantified forecast skill objectively

Key challenges remain in:
- Convective initiation prediction and sufficient warning lead times
- High-resolution data assimilation and probabilistic nowcasting
- AI and machine learning integration
- Fire weather, complex terrain, and urban applications
- Reaching developing countries with limited observational infrastructure

The WMO Early Warning for All initiative (target: warnings in every country by 2027) requires both the physical science of nowcasting and communication/preparedness along the complete science-to-services value chain.
