## Overview

This 25-page review article summarizes insights gained from Forecast Demonstration Projects (FDPs), Research and Development Projects (RDPs), training workshops, and symposia organized by the WMO World Weather Research Programme's Nowcasting and Mesoscale Research Working Group from 2000 to 2024. The projects were associated with Olympic Games, emergency management, and aviation services, and were designed to advance the science of nowcasting and very short-range forecasting (VSRF, 0–6 h). The paper distills lessons on end-user decision-making, forecast system design, NWP modeling, verification, and technology transfer accumulated across 25 years of international collaboration.

## Context and Motivation

Nowcasting and VSRF have long been identified as critical for severe weather warnings, yet warning lead times have remained stubbornly short (average 8–10 min for tornadoes) with little improvement over two decades. The WMO WWRP, founded in 1998, created the NMR Working Group to bridge the gap between observation-based extrapolation systems (0–2 h) and mesoscale NWP (48 h to <2 h). High-profile events like the Olympics provided structured opportunities to bring international expertise together, test state-of-the-art nowcasting systems under operational conditions, and accelerate research-to-operations transfer.

## Key Scientific Contributions

- Documents 25 years of progress in nowcasting from pure observation-based extrapolation to blended NWP-observation, stochastic ensemble, and AI-based systems
- Synthesizes lessons on end-user decision-making: decisions are complex, not purely meteorology-driven, and nowcasting information was initially underestimated but became indispensable
- Establishes the FDP/RDP model as an effective mechanism for technology transfer, with the "early adopter" framework explaining how research-to-operations transitions succeed
- Documents the expansion of nowcasting scope from summer convection to winter weather, complex terrain, urban environments, aviation, air quality, hydrology, and health
- Identifies persistent science gaps: convective initiation, high-resolution data assimilation, probabilistic nowcasting, AI integration, and urban boundary layer processes

## Methods Summary

A narrative review, organized by project (Table 1) and theme (section 4: Insights). Projects span 2000–2024 and include FDPs associated with Sydney 2000 (S2K), Beijing 2008 (B08), Vancouver 2010 (V2010), Sochi 2014 (FROST-2014), PyeongChang 2018 (ICE-POP), and interdisciplinary urban/aviation projects (INCA-CE, TOMACS, ECPASS, AvRDP). Insights are distilled from the collective experience of participants and documented in individual project overview papers; this paper focuses on the intangible cross-cutting lessons.

## Key Results

- Nowcast systems progressed from km-scale radar extrapolation to hectometric NWP, real-time blending with observations, and AI-based predictions.
- FDPs successfully accelerated technology transfer; all high-resolution research products were adopted operationally after demonstration.
- Verification was essential and challenging: high-resolution systems (5–10 min, few km) were disadvantaged by coarse verification networks and hourly surface observations.
- The scope of nowcasting services expanded from summer thunderstorm warnings to winter, aviation, urban, and integrated environmental services.
- Persistent challenges include: convective initiation prediction, adequate warning lead times, affordable observation networks, and effective risk communication in developing countries.
- The WMO Early Warning for All initiative (target: every country with warnings by 2027) requires the full science-to-services value chain.

## Limitations and Caveats

- As a narrative review, insights are qualitative and based on participant experience rather than systematic synthesis of quantitative results; individual project papers provide quantitative outcomes.
- Priorities and recommendations for future directions are covered in the separate WWRP implementation plan (WWRP 2024c) rather than in this paper.
- The paper uses pre-2019 WMO department names for consistency with historical documentation, even though WMO underwent major reform in 2019.

## Relation to Author's Research Program

Milbrandt is the 28th of 38 co-authors, all from the institutions that participated in the NMR-endorsed FDPs and RDPs over 25 years. His role reflects ECCC's broader involvement in WMO nowcasting initiatives, most directly through the Vancouver 2010 Winter Olympics FDP/RDP (V2010), in which ECCC contributed an experimental high-resolution forecast system using the GEM model with advanced microphysics (cited in this paper as Mailhot et al. 2014 ⚠ verify). This paper is relevant to Milbrandt's work insofar as it documents the broader applied context — Olympic and emergency management FDPs — in which ECCC's high-resolution NWP capabilities (including P3/MY microphysics) were deployed and demonstrated operationally.
